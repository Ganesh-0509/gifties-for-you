# 08. Typography and Color Strategy

> Directional only, per Stage 1 rules (no hex values, no final font selection). To be finalized in Stage 2 against confirmed brand assets (logo, real product photos).

## Color Strategy

### Base Surface Direction
Warm neutral, not stark white and not dark/moody — gifting content (photos of products, packaging, colors) needs a calm, light background that doesn't compete with product photography. Direction only; exact tone depends on real product photo palette, which is UNKNOWN.

### Primary Accent Purpose
Should carry the "warm, personal, occasion-aware" personality identified in `07-visual-direction.md` — a color that reads as gift-appropriate (celebratory but not garish) rather than a generic brand-blue or SaaS-purple. Exact hue: not finalized — should be chosen once any existing brand color (logo, packaging) is confirmed. UNKNOWN whether the client already has a color identity.

### Secondary Accent
Reserved for occasion/category differentiation if the catalogue ends up organized by multiple occasion types (wedding vs. birthday vs. corporate, etc.) — TBD, depends on confirmed product range.

### Semantic Colors
Minimal need — no cart/checkout/payment states in this scope. Only needed for: form validation (if a fallback enquiry form is built), and enquiry-sent confirmation feedback.

### Colors To Avoid Dominating
- Aggressive discount-red/sale-badge styling by default (seen across Tanu/Pai by Lee/Wedtree) — only justified if the client actually wants a promotions-driven storefront, which is UNKNOWN.
- Purple/blue AI-SaaS gradients — not appropriate for this product regardless.
- Pure white or pure black backgrounds as the dominant surface — doesn't suit warm gift photography.

## Typography Strategy

### Typography Personality
Should feel personal and warm rather than corporate/technical — gifting is an emotional purchase category. A pairing that avoids both "generic default UI sans" and "overly ornate wedding-invite script" is likely right, but this is a Stage 2 decision once brand direction is validated with the client.

### Heading Style
Should carry some warmth/character (this is a differentiator opportunity vs. the generic e-commerce feel of the 4 references) without becoming decorative/hard to read at small sizes.

### Body Style
Highly readable, mobile-first — most traffic will be mobile (Instagram-referred, WhatsApp-adjacent audience), so body text needs to hold up at small sizes and in bright outdoor lighting conditions (a real consideration for India-based mobile browsing).

### Numerical Typography
Prices need to be clearly legible and easy to scan across a grid of products — this matters more here than in a typical content site, since price-based browsing is a confirmed market pattern across all 4 references.

### Monospace
Not required — no code, no technical data in this product.

### Density
Low-to-medium — this is a small catalogue, not a dense data product; typography and spacing should feel closer to editorial/lifestyle content than to a dense retail grid.
