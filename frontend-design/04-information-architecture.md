# 04. Information Architecture

## Navigation Model
**Top navigation, mobile-first, shallow (2 levels max)** — not a mega-menu (Wedtree-scale mega-menus assume 5000+ SKUs; this is a small local shop with a simple-site scope), not a sidebar (this is a browsing/catalogue site, not a workspace tool), not search-first (catalogue is likely small enough that browsing beats search, pending confirmation of actual product count).

## Navigation Reasoning
The CONFIRMED scope is a simple catalogue + WhatsApp enquiry site — no accounts, no cart, no checkout. That rules out account/cart icons in the header (unlike all 4 references, which are full e-commerce sites). Navigation should optimize for the two things the market research shows customers actually look for first: **occasion** and **price** — both are used across every reference site (Pai by Lee and Wedtree lead with price brackets; Thambulaaa and Wedtree lead with occasion/category). Which of these Gifties For You should lead with depends on their actual product range — UNKNOWN — NEEDS CONFIRMATION.

## Primary Navigation (draft — pending real category data)
- Home
- Shop / Catalogue (by category — actual categories UNKNOWN)
- About
- Contact / Enquire

## Secondary Navigation
- Occasion or price filters live *within* the catalogue page, not as top-level nav items — keeps the header shallow while still supporting the market-standard browsing patterns (by occasion, by price, by product type).

## Contextual Navigation
- On a product detail view: back to catalogue, related/similar products (if catalogue is large enough to support this — UNKNOWN), and the WhatsApp enquiry action (primary, always visible).
- Sticky/floating WhatsApp contact affordance is a strong market pattern worth adopting (Thambulaaa, Wedtree, and Tanu all foreground WhatsApp or phone contact) — recommended, not yet confirmed with client.

## Search
Only worth including if the catalogue is large enough to need it. UNKNOWN — NEEDS CONFIRMATION (depends on real product count, which we don't have).

## Filtering
By occasion and/or price bracket, following the market pattern — but the actual filter dimensions depend entirely on the real product data, which is UNKNOWN.
